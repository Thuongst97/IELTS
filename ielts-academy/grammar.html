Grammar for Band 7

Band 7 grammar = a variety of complex structures + frequent error-free sentences. You need both: complex sentences full of mistakes stay at 6, and accurate simple sentences stay at 6 too.

🧩 8 structure groups✅ 15-question error-correction quiz🇻🇳 Notes on Vietnamese interference

7.1Grammatical range for Band 7

Examiners look at the mix of sentence types and the proportion of sentences with no errors at all.

Band 6

Mix of simple and complex sentences, but complex ones often contain errors. Errors rarely stop communication.

Band 7 ✦

Variety of complex structures (subordinate clauses, conditionals, relative clauses, passives) used with some flexibility; frequent error-free sentences; good control of punctuation.

Band 8

Wide range, used flexibly. The majority of sentences are error-free; only occasional slips.

🎯
A practical target

In a 280-word essay (about 14 sentences): at least 8–9 completely error-free sentences, using at least 5 different complex structures. Keep sentences under ~35 words — long sentences multiply errors.

7.2Complex sentence toolkit

A complex sentence has a main clause plus at least one dependent clause. These are the most useful types for IELTS:

StructureExample
Contrast: although / while / whereasAlthough online learning is flexible, it requires considerable self-discipline.
Cause: because / since / asSince housing costs have soared, many young adults still live with their parents.
Result: so … that / such … thatTraffic has become so heavy that commuting by car is no longer practical.
Purpose: so that / in order toCities should invest in cycle lanes so that residents can travel safely.
Time: once / until / as soon asOnce students graduate, they often struggle to find stable jobs.
Noun clause: what / whether / the fact thatWhat concerns many parents is the fact that children spend hours online.
Cleft sentence (emphasis)It is the cost of housing, not the lack of jobs, that drives young people away.
Comparative structureThe more cities expand, the longer people spend commuting.
Inversion (formal, use rarely)Not only does exercise improve health, but it also reduces stress.

7.3Tenses that matter in IELTS

TenseWhere you need itExample
Present simpleGeneral truths, Task 2 arguments, process diagramsPublic transport reduces congestion.
Present perfectChanges up to now — very common in Task 2 introductions and SpeakingCar ownership has risen dramatically in recent decades.
Past simpleTask 1 graphs with past dates; Speaking Part 2 storiesThe figure fell to 21,000 in 2025.
Past perfectAn earlier past event — great in Part 2 storiesI had never driven a car before I started lessons.
Future formsTask 1 projections; Speaking Part 3 predictionsThe population is expected to reach 10 million by 2040.
Present perfect continuousOngoing situationsI have been studying English for six years.
🇻🇳
Vietnamese interference

Vietnamese marks time with words (đã, đang, sẽ) and never changes the verb. In English the verb itself must change: Yesterday I go → went; She work → works. Check every verb in your writing for tense and the 3rd-person -s.

7.4Conditionals & hypotheticals

TypeFormExample
Zero (facts)if + present, presentIf people exercise regularly, they sleep better.
First (real future)if + present, willIf the government raises fuel taxes, fewer people will drive.
Second (hypothetical)if + past, wouldIf university were free, more students from poor families would enrol.
Third (past hypothetical)if + past perfect, would haveIf I had started earlier, I would have passed the first time.
Mixedif + past perfect, would + verbIf cities had invested in metro systems, traffic would be lighter today.
Alternatives to “if”unless, provided that, as long asThe policy will fail unless it is properly funded.

💡 Second conditionals are perfect for Task 2 solutions (“If schools taught financial skills, young people would …”) and Speaking Part 3 speculation.

7.5Relative & participle clauses

Relative clauses

  • Defining (no commas): Students who work part-time often have less time to study.
  • Non-defining (commas): Hanoi, which has a population of over 8 million, faces severe congestion.
  • Sentence relative: Many graduates move abroad, which leads to a shortage of skilled workers.
  • Preposition + which: the extent to which, the way in which.

❌ Never use that after a comma: Hanoi, that is the capital…

Participle clauses (very Band 7+)

  • -ing (active): Living far from the city centre, many workers commute for hours.
  • -ed (passive): Built in the 1960s, the bridge now needs major repairs.
  • Result: The figure climbed sharply, reaching a peak of 46,000.
  • Having + past participle: Having considered both sides, I believe…

⚠️ The subject must match: Walking to school, the rain started. (The rain wasn't walking!)

7.6Passive & impersonal structures

The passive is essential in Task 1 processes and maps and gives Task 2 an objective, academic tone.

UseExample
Process diagramsThe beans are harvested and then dried in the sun before they are roasted.
Maps (changes)The old factory was demolished and has been replaced by apartments.
Impersonal opinionIt is widely believed that technology isolates people.
Reported beliefExercise is thought to reduce the risk of depression.
Modal passive (solutions)Stricter laws should be introduced to protect consumers.

7.7Articles, countability & agreement

Vietnamese has no articles and no plural endings, so these are the most frequent errors for Vietnamese candidates. A simplified rule set:

Articles in 5 rules

  1. a/an + singular countable noun mentioned for the first time / one of many: a teacher.
  2. the when the reader knows which one: the government (of your country), the graph, the internet.
  3. no article for general plural / uncountable nouns: The people need the education.
  4. the + superlatives and ordinals: the highest, the first.
  5. no article for abstract ideas in general: society, technology, nature, education.

Countability & agreement

  • Uncountable — no -s, singular verb: information, advice, research, equipment, knowledge, evidence, traffic, furniture.
  • The number of + plural noun + singular verb: The number of cars is rising.
  • A number of + plural noun + plural verb: A number of studies have shown…
  • People / police / children are plural: People are…
  • Everyone / each / every + singular verb: Every student needs…

7.8Punctuation that examiners notice

Rule✗✓
No comma splicesPrices rose, demand fell.Prices rose, so demand fell. / Prices rose; demand fell.
Comma after a fronted clauseAlthough it is cheap many people avoid it.Although it is cheap, many people avoid it.
Comma after sentence linkersHowever many people disagree.However, many people disagree.
“However” between clausesIt is useful, however it is expensive.It is useful; however, it is expensive.
No comma before “that” clausesMany argue, that…Many argue that…
