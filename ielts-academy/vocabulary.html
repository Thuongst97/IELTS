9.1What Band 7 vocabulary means

Precision

Choosing the word that says exactly what you mean: problems → congestion, bad → detrimental, change → revolutionise.

Collocation

Words that naturally go together: pose a threat (not make a threat), heavy traffic (not big traffic), raise awareness.

Less common items

Topic-specific and idiomatic language used correctly: urban sprawl, a sedentary lifestyle, a deterrent.

⚠️
The “thesaurus trap”

Replacing simple words with rare synonyms you don't fully know lowers your score: The utilisation of automobiles has escalated exponentially. A Band 7 writer says: Car use has risen sharply. Learn every new word with its collocations and an example — never as a single item.

9.4Paraphrasing techniques

Paraphrasing is tested in Reading and Listening and rewarded in Writing (introductions) and Speaking. Combine two or more techniques for a natural result:

TechniqueOriginalParaphrase
1. SynonymsMany people think that children should…Many people believe that young people should…
2. Word formTechnology has developed rapidly.There has been rapid technological development.
3. Active ↔ passiveGovernments should fund public transport.Public transport should be funded by the state.
4. Clause structurePeople live longer, so there are more elderly people.As a result of increased life expectancy, the proportion of elderly people is growing.
5. General ↔ specificWatching TV, playing games and using phones…Screen-based activities…

💡
Reference beats linkers

Words like this, these, such, the former/the latter, it, they, one link sentences more naturally than “Moreover” and are specifically mentioned in the Band 7 descriptor (“reference and substitution”). Prices have soared. This has made such housing unaffordable for those on average incomes.
