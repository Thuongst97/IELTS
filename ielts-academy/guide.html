Learning Guidelines: Your Path to 7.0

What to study, in what order, and how to know when you are ready to move on. Each skill has three stages — from about 6.0 to a consistent 7.0 — and every stage tells you exactly which lessons and which of the 10 practice tests to use.

🗺️ 3 stages per skill📝 Linked to Practice Tests 1–10✅ Saved milestone checklist

2.1How to use these guidelines

  1. Find your stage for each skillTake one Listening and one Reading test (Practice Test 1), write one Task 2 essay and record one Part 2 answer. Then use the bands below. Most learners are at different stages in different skills — that is normal.
  2. Work on 2 skills intensively, 2 lightlySpend about two-thirds of your time on your two weakest skills, while keeping the other two active with short daily practice.
  3. Move on only when you meet the exit targetEach stage ends with a measurable target (“Move on when…”). If you don't meet it, repeat the stage with different material rather than rushing ahead.
  4. Review more than you practiseA test you analyse carefully is worth three tests you only score. Keep a mistake log and look for patterns.

2.2Foundation: vocabulary & grammar

Vocabulary and grammar are not separate exam papers, but they decide your score in all four. Keep them going every day, alongside the skill paths below.

Daily
Vocabulary
15 min
Do
  • Review the flashcards that are due (spaced repetition) — never skip a day.
  • Learn each word with its collocation and an example, never alone.
  • Add 5 useful words a day from your Reading and Listening practice.
  • Say new words aloud (🔊 buttons) and check the stressed syllable.
Use
  • Topic vocabulary — one topic per week
  • Collocations and paraphrasing
  • Linking words for Writing & Speaking
Target: all 120 items in box 4+ (learned) and used in at least one essay or speaking answer.
Weekly
Grammar
2 × 30 min
Do
  • Study one grammar section per week, in order: articles & agreement → tenses → complex sentences → relative & participle clauses → conditionals → passive → punctuation.
  • Write 5 sentences of your own with each new structure, about IELTS topics.
  • Find the same errors in your own essays — this is where improvement happens.
Use
  • Error-correction quiz (repeat until 14/15)
  • Common errors for Vietnamese learners
Target: no “Although… but”, “Because… so”, uncountable -s or comma-splice errors in your last three essays.

2.3🎧 Listening study path

Listening improves through accuracy first, then stamina. Most marks lost at 6.0–6.5 are in Parts 1–2, on spelling, numbers and distractors.

Stage 1
Accuracy foundations
6.023–25 / 40
Do
  • Learn every question type and its method (2.2) and the five distractor traps (2.3).
  • Daily: 10 minutes of the dictation trainer — spelling, numbers, dates, postcodes.
  • Keep a personal spelling list of every word you misspell.
  • Do Practice Tests 1–2 Listening part by part, with Exam mode off: replay difficult sections, then read the transcript.
Use
  • Practice Test 1 · Listening
  • Practice Test 2 · Listening
Move on when: dictation ≥ 90% correct and Part 1 ≥ 9/10 in two tests.
Stage 2
Following longer talk
6.526–29 / 40
Do
  • Practice Tests 3–6 in Exam mode (played once, 30 s reading time). One part a day on weekdays; a full test at the weekend.
  • After every test, mark the answer locations in the transcript and log each mistake by cause: spelling · distractor · lost place · paraphrase · plural.
  • Keep a paraphrase notebook: question wording ↔ speaker wording.
  • Daily: 10 minutes of shadowing (BBC 6 Minute English) to train linking and weak forms.
Use
  • Practice Tests 3 · 4 · 5 · 6
  • Band 7 training routine
Move on when: 27+ / 40 in two consecutive tests in Exam mode.
Stage 3
Band 7 consistency
7.030+ / 40
Do
  • Practice Tests 7–10 as full mocks: Exam mode, all four parts in one sitting, speed 0.95× or 1.05×.
  • Focus on Parts 3–4: predict the type of each answer during reading time; practise note-taking from TED talks.
  • Build a 2-minute end-of-test check: spelling, plurals, word limits, capital letters for names.
  • Add Cambridge IELTS 15–19 tests (official audio, variety of accents) and mark them with the answer sheets.
Use
  • Practice Tests 7 · 8 · 9 · 10
Ready when: 30+ / 40 in three consecutive full tests.

2.4📖 Reading study path

Reading at 7.0 is about method and timing: locate quickly, read the key sentences carefully, and never confuse FALSE with NOT GIVEN.

Stage 1
Method before speed
6.023–26 / 40
Do
  • Learn the method for each question type (3.3) and the 5-step passage method (3.2).
  • Master the decision rule with the TFNG trainer.
  • Do the passages of Practice Tests 1–2 untimed. For every answer, highlight the sentence that proves it.
  • Daily: one article (20 min); write a one-line summary of each paragraph — this trains matching headings.
Use
  • Practice Test 1 · Reading
  • Practice Test 2 · Reading
  • Paraphrase recognition
Move on when: TFNG trainer 6/6 and one passage finished in under 25 minutes with 9+/13.
Stage 2
Timed passages
6.527–29 / 40
Do
  • Practice Tests 3–6: one passage at a time, strictly 20 minutes each.
  • Log every mistake by question type; find your weakest type and do extra practice on it.
  • Never spend more than 90 seconds on one question — guess, mark it, return later.
  • Collect paraphrase pairs from every passage (passage word ↔ question word).
Use
  • Practice Tests 3 · 4 · 5 · 6
Move on when: 10+/13 per passage in 20 minutes, three times in a row.
Stage 3
Full-test performance
7.030+ / 40
Do
  • Practice Tests 7–10 as full 60-minute tests (17 / 20 / 23 minutes per passage).
  • Practise writer's-view questions (YES/NO/NOT GIVEN) — usually Passage 3.
  • Leave 2 minutes at the end: no blank answers, check spelling of copied words.
  • Read one Guardian Long Read or The Conversation article a week — Passage 3 style.
Use
  • Practice Tests 7 · 8 · 9 · 10
Ready when: 30+ / 40 in three consecutive full tests within 60 minutes.
ℹ️
About the practice passages

The passages in Practice Tests 2–10 are a little shorter than real IELTS passages (about 450–550 words versus 700–900). Treat 60 minutes as comfortable on these tests; for realistic timing pressure, add Cambridge IELTS tests in Stage 3.

2.5✍️ Writing study path

Writing usually takes the longest to improve. The path is: structure → development → accuracy. Feedback from a teacher or a strong study partner speeds this up more than anything else.

Stage 1
Structure & clarity
6.0
Do
  • Learn the four criteria and the Band 7 column (4.1).
  • Task 1: write only the introduction + overview for all ten Task 1 charts in the practice tests (10 × 10 minutes). An overview is the single biggest Task 1 improvement.
  • Task 2: learn the five essay structures (4.6); write one essay a week, untimed, and compare it with a model (4.7).
  • Remove the top errors (4.9) from every essay.
Use
  • Task 1 charts: Test 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 …
  • Task 1 language bank
Move on when: every Task 1 has a number-free overview, and every essay has 4–5 paragraphs and one clear position.
Stage 2
Developed ideas, timed
6.5
Do
  • Two timed Task 2 essays a week (40 min) in the Writing studio; one timed Task 1 (20 min).
  • Use PEEL for every body paragraph: point → explanation → example → link.
  • After writing, open the task's Band 7 plan in the practice test and check you covered every part of the question.
  • Rewrite one essay a week using the Band 6 → 7 upgrades.
Use
  • Writing papers of Practice Tests 6–8
  • “🔍 Analyse my writing” in the studio
Move on when: you finish both tasks inside 60 minutes, at length, and your self-assessment is 6.5+ on all four criteria.
Stage 3
Accuracy & range
7.0
Do
  • Full 60-minute writing papers from Practice Tests 9–10 and Cambridge books (with the Writing room).
  • Get at least 4 essays marked by a teacher against the band descriptors; fix the specific errors they find.
  • Target 8–9 completely error-free sentences per essay, and at least 5 different complex structures.
  • Use topic collocations naturally; avoid memorised phrases.
Use
  • Practice Test 9 · Practice Test 10
  • Grammar for Band 7
Ready when: three essays in a row are marked 7 (by a teacher, or by strict self-assessment with the model answers).

2.6🎙️ Speaking study path

Speaking improves only by speaking. Record yourself every day, even for five minutes, and listen back — that is how you hear your own fluency, endings and errors.

Stage 1
Confident short answers
6.0
Do
  • Part 1 with the formula answer → reason → detail (5.2). Practise all 14 topics in the question bank.
  • Daily: pronunciation drills (5.5) — final consonants, -s/-ed endings, word stress.
  • Record Part 1 sets in the Speaking studio and count long pauses.
Use
  • Band 7 language toolkit
  • Part 1 of Practice Tests 1–3
Move on when: you answer any Part 1 question for 15–25 seconds without a long pause.
Stage 2
The long turn & discussion
6.5
Do
  • Part 2: three cue cards a week with 1-minute keyword notes and a full 2-minute talk (5.3).
  • Part 3: the 4-step framework — direct answer, reason, example, balance (5.4).
  • Learn 3–4 phrases from each group of the language toolkit and use them until they feel natural.
  • Turn on the live transcript and reduce filler words.
Use
  • Speaking tests in Practice Tests 4–7
Move on when: you can speak for the full 2 minutes on any cue card, and give 30-second Part 3 answers.
Stage 3
Natural, accurate, flexible
7.0
Do
  • Full speaking tests from Practice Tests 8–10, recorded from start to finish; score yourself on the four criteria.
  • At least 2–3 mock tests with a teacher or a partner who can play the examiner.
  • Listen for errors in complex sentences and correct them in a re-recording.
  • Talk about abstract topics daily (society, technology, education) — Part 3 decides 6.5 vs 7.
Use
  • Practice Test 8 · 9 · 10
Ready when: recordings show fluent 2-minute talks, few grammar slips and clear endings — and a teacher's mock gives 7.
