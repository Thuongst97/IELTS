Module 03 · Reading

Reading: 30/40 and Above

Academic Reading is a test of locating and interpreting information under time pressure — not of understanding every word. Band 7 needs 30 correct answers out of 40 in 60 minutes, with no extra transfer time.

⏱ 60 minutes · 3 passages🎯 Academic: 30/40 · General Training: 34/40📄 ~2,700 words in total

3.1Format & Band 7 target

Academic Reading

  • 3 long passages from books, journals, magazines and newspapers, written for a non-specialist audience.
  • Difficulty increases: Passage 3 is usually argumentative, with the writer's opinions tested (YES/NO/NOT GIVEN).
  • Band 7 ≈ 30/40 (7.5 ≈ 33/40).

General Training Reading

  • Section 1: short everyday texts (notices, adverts). Section 2: work-related texts. Section 3: one longer general-interest text.
  • The texts are easier, so the conversion is stricter.
  • Band 7 ≈ 34/40.
🎯
Where Band 7 candidates lose marks

Not on difficult vocabulary — on NOT GIVEN vs FALSE, on matching headings, and on running out of time in Passage 3. This module trains all three.

3.2Time management

There is no transfer time in Reading, so you must finish inside 60 minutes. Use a fixed budget and never spend more than 90 seconds on one question — guess, mark it, move on, and return at the end.

PassageBudgetWhat to do
Passage 117 minUsually the most factual. Bank time here.
Passage 220 minOften has matching headings / matching information.
Passage 323 minHardest; writer's views. Includes 1–2 minutes to check that every answer is filled in.

A method that works for every passage

  1. Skim the passage (2–3 min)Read the title, the first and last sentence of each paragraph. Ask: what is each paragraph for? Write a 3-word summary in the margin.
  2. Read the first question groupUnderline keywords — especially names, numbers, dates and capitalised terms that are easy to scan for (they can't be paraphrased).
  3. Scan to locate, then read intensivelyFind the area of the text, then read 2–3 sentences around it slowly and carefully. Most mistakes happen when students answer from the scan without reading carefully.
  4. Use question orderGap fill, TFNG and MCQ answers follow the order of the passage. Headings, matching information and matching features do not.
  5. Never leave a blankThere is no penalty for wrong answers.

3.3Question types & tactics

The decision rule

  • TRUE The passage says the same thing in different words.
  • FALSE The passage says the opposite — or something that cannot be true at the same time.
  • NOT GIVEN The passage doesn't say enough to decide. The topic may be mentioned, but this specific claim isn't.

YES/NO/NOT GIVEN is identical but tests the writer's opinions, not facts.

Traps to check

  • Qualifiers: all / some / most / always / usually / only. “Some historians believe” ≠ “Historians have proven”.
  • Comparisons: “more than”, “the first”, “the largest” — is the comparison really in the text?
  • Swapped subjects: who did what? (Beavers use castoreum to mark territory — humans don't.)
  • Your own knowledge: if the passage doesn't say it, it's NOT GIVEN, even if it's true in real life.

Method

  1. Do this task first if it comes first — it gives you a map of the passage.
  2. Read the paragraph, then the headings. Write your own 3–5 word summary before looking.
  3. Focus on the main idea, usually in the first or second sentence, but watch for “However…” that turns the paragraph.
  4. Start with the paragraphs you find easiest; eliminate used headings.

Why wrong headings look right

Distractor headings use a word from a detail in the paragraph. In Passage 2 below, paragraph D mentions social media, but the heading “How technology has made the problem worse” is wrong — social media is just an example of an immediate reward.

Matching information (“Which paragraph contains…”)

  • The hardest type for time: answers are not in order and the information is a detail, not the main idea.
  • Decode the type of information asked: a reference to…, an example of…, a comparison…, a description of…, a reason for….
  • Leave it until after the other question groups on the same passage — by then you know the text well.
  • A paragraph can be used more than once (the instructions say “You may use any letter more than once”).

Summary, note, table and flow-chart completion

  • Check the word limit. “ONE WORD ONLY” means exactly one word — a disease is wrong.
  • Predict the part of speech of each gap from the grammar around it.
  • Copy the word exactly as spelled in the passage, in the form that fits the sentence.
  • With a word box (A–H), the answer is usually a synonym of the passage word: “first moment” → initial.

Multiple choice

  • Locate using the stem, then read the relevant lines carefully before looking at the options.
  • Each wrong option is wrong for a reason: true but doesn't answer the question, uses passage words but changes meaning, from a different paragraph, or contradicted.
  • Questions about the writer's purpose (“in order to…”, “the writer mentions X to…”) need the function of the sentence, not its content.

Matching sentence endings

  • First eliminate endings that don't fit grammatically (singular/plural verbs, tense).
  • Questions follow passage order; locate each beginning, then decide.
  • Two extra endings are distractors — often true statements about the topic that the passage never says.

3.5Paraphrase recognition

The core Band 7 skill: recognising the same idea in different words. These are the five ways IELTS paraphrases a passage.

TechniquePassageQuestion
Synonyma handful of isolated populationsa small number of separate locations
Word formthe accessibility of memorieshow accessible memories are
Active ↔ passiveecologists describe beavers as…beavers are described as…
Negative ↔ positiveadvice to “just try harder” is unlikely to helpeffort alone is ineffective
Generalisationinsects, amphibians, fish and birdswildlife

High-frequency paraphrase pairs

Change & trend

  • rise = increase, grow, climb, surge
  • decline = fall, drop, decrease, diminish
  • remain stable = level off, plateau
  • substantial = considerable, significant, marked

Cause & result

  • cause = lead to, result in, give rise to, trigger
  • due to = owing to, as a result of, stem from
  • affect = influence, have an impact on
  • prevent = stop, hinder, inhibit

Opinion & evidence

  • claim = argue, maintain, assert
  • prove = demonstrate, confirm, establish
  • suggest = indicate, imply
  • doubt = question, be sceptical of

Quantity & frequency

  • most = the majority, a large proportion
  • few = a minority, a handful
  • often = frequently, regularly, tend to
  • rarely = seldom, hardly ever

💡
Review is where improvement happens

For every wrong answer, find the sentence that proves the correct answer and ask: Did I find the right place? Did I misread a qualifier? Did I use my own knowledge? Students who do this consistently gain 3–5 marks in a month.
